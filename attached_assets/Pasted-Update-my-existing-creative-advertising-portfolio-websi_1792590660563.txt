## Section structure

### 1. Campaign introduction
Create a striking opening section with:
- Campaign title
- Brand name
- Campaign category
- A short campaign description
- A clear label stating **“Independent Speculative Campaign — Not commissioned or endorsed by the brand.”**

Use placeholder text that I can easily replace later. Do not invent a brand, campaign results, research findings, or statistics.

### 2. The Company
Create a text-based section introducing the selected company.

Include editable fields for:
- Company and industry overview
- Main products or services
- Target audience
- Brand positioning and identity
- Relevant background about the company

Present this information in an engaging editorial layout with readable text, highlighted keywords, and small supporting visual elements where appropriate.

### 3. The Problem
Create a separate text-based section explaining the problem or opportunity the campaign addresses.

Include editable fields for:
- The current challenge faced by the brand
- Evidence or sources supporting the challenge
- The target audience's needs, behaviour, or frustrations
- Why the problem matters
- The opportunity for advertising to address it

Visually distinguish documented facts from my own observations or interpretations. Include a small source/reference area that I can fill in. Do not fabricate facts, statistics, citations, or claims about the company.

### 4. Creative Insight and Strategy
Add a concise bridge between the problem and the creative work.

Include editable fields for:
- Audience insight
- Core campaign idea
- Key message
- Desired audience response

Use a visually distinctive layout, such as a large quotation-style insight statement with supporting text.

### 5. Campaign Poster
Create a prominent visual showcase for the campaign poster.

Requirements:
- A large poster preview area with an image-upload or image-replacement placeholder.
- Space for the headline, supporting copy, tagline, and call to action.
- A short explanation of the poster's creative rationale.
- A layout that lets me upload my own poster later without breaking the design.
- Allow the poster to be clicked to view it larger, if practical.

Do not generate a fake finished poster. Use a clearly labelled placeholder until I add my own design.

### 6. Advertisement Storyboard
Create a storyboard section that displays advertisement frames in sequence.

Each frame should have:
- Frame number
- Image or sketch placeholder
- Visual description
- Dialogue, voice-over, or on-screen text
- Camera direction or movement, where relevant
- Sound or music notes
- Approximate duration

Use a clean storyboard grid or horizontal sequence. Make the frames easy to duplicate and edit. Include a field for the intended total advertisement duration.

Do not create fictional finished frames; use labelled placeholders.

### 7. Final campaign summary
At the end, include:
- Campaign objective
- Creative approach
- Deliverables
- Skills demonstrated
- A note that the project is speculative and independently developed

Only display information I have entered. Do not invent campaign outcomes or claim that the advertisement was published.
